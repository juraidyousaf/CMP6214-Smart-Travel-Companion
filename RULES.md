# Project rules

These rules apply to every phase. The assessment brief and the Week 5 template are the source of the integrity requirements (Theil, 2026; Week 5 template, 2026). If a file in this workspace conflicts with the brief or with written guidance from your lecturer, follow the brief and the lecturer.

## 1. Your work, your evidence

You author the assessed submissions. That includes the introduction, market research, competitor analysis, personas, jobs-to-be-done, journey maps, MoSCoW requirements, HTAs, the reference list, the prototype’s design rationale, and the usability report.

A template, outline, or plan is not a completed deliverable.

## 2. Do not fabricate

The following are not to be invented, simulated, or reconstructed by AI or by guesswork:

- Interviews, survey responses, or participant details
- Quotations from users
- Research findings and statistics
- Citations and reference-list entries for sources you have not opened
- Accessibility needs presented as lived experience
- Usability-test results, scores, or comments
- Completed personas, journey maps, requirements, or HTA diagrams offered as your research

If a task needs people, consent, or academic approval, the task stays open. Write the action in `PHASES.md` or `MEMORY.md`. Do not fill the gap with fictional completion.

## 3. Label every claim

| Label | Use |
|---|---|
| VERIFIED | You read or observed it, and the source register has an ID |
| HYPOTHESIS | Plausible, not yet supported |
| ILLUSTRATIVE | Shows a method or a format. Not evidence about tourists |
| INCOMPLETE | Waiting for you |
| BLOCKED | Waiting for approval or for access you do not have |

Verified material, hypotheses, examples, and unfinished sections stay visually separate in every document.

## 4. AI assistance

The brief allows AI with conditions. AI may support UI ideas, research organisation, design critique, formatting, and technical documentation. AI must not replace your design ownership, thinking, or analysis, and generated content must not be copied into a submission without your judgement.

If you use generative AI, the submission declaration must state:

- why you used it
- what it was used for
- which system was used
- whether any generated content appears directly in the submission, and where

That declaration does not count toward the 2,000-word report. Keep records in `AI_USAGE_LOG.md`. A missing or misleading AI declaration is academic misconduct under the brief.

You should be able to explain how UX principles, laws, and heuristics informed your decisions. Explanations in the Week 9 demo and the report must be yours.

## 5. Research evidence standard

A persona, job, journey step, or requirement is ready for the checklist only when all of these are true:

1. It links to one or more source IDs you verified.
2. The underlying note is in your words, or a quotation is copied exactly with a citation and access date.
3. Assumptions are still labelled as assumptions.
4. You can explain it without reading from an AI draft.
5. Where the method replaces interviews, record the permission. On 18 October 2026 you confirmed that secondary research may inform the initial user-centred design work. No lecturer email is filed. Label that permission user-reported until the document is stored. Phase 3 participant testing is not covered by this confirmation.

Public web pages, app-store reviews, and academic papers are sources only after you open them and log them. A search result title is not a source. An abstract-only source stays marked that way. A page that failed to load is Unverified and is not cited.

Secondary research may organise the Week 5 argument. It may not be written up as if you had interviewed people. AI-opened sources in the register still need your own reading before they enter the Word file. The brief still forbids using AI to create personas, journey maps, requirements, HTA flowcharts, references, or research results.

## 6. Citations

The brief requires **Cite Them Right Harvard**.

BCU Library’s Harvard page, opened 18 October 2026, says Harvard is the university’s main referencing system and that “BCU Harvard is being phased out but may still be used on your course.” The assessment brief is the marking instruction: use Cite Them Right Harvard. Guides are on the BCU Library referencing pages and require a BCU sign-in for the PDFs:

- https://www.bcu.ac.uk/library/services-and-support/referencing
- https://www.bcu.ac.uk/library/services-and-support/referencing/Harvard

Before you cite:

- Open the source.
- Record author, year, title, and location in `research/source-register.md`.
- Add the access date for online material.
- Check the reference shape against the current BCU Harvard guide, not against memory.

Do not add a source to the submission reference list until the register marks it verified by you.

## 7. Accessibility

The brief requires one persona with some form of impairment or disability, and mobile accessibility considerations in that persona and in the prototypes.

Rules for this project:

- You choose the impairment from evidence you can actually gather. Secondary sources can inform that choice. They cannot supply a fictional biography.
- Do not assign a disability in order to complete the template.
- Do not describe how a disabled person experiences the city unless a source you cite says so, and then paraphrase or quote that source.
- Accessibility requirements enter the MoSCoW list only when they are justified.
- Prototype work later needs to show the relevant features in paper sketches and in Figma, not only in a paragraph.

WCAG 2.2 is the standard to consult when you reach visual design and prototyping: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ . Read it and log the access date before you cite it. Meeting a guideline is a design aim until you have checked the screens.

## 8. Scope limits

- No application code is required.
- No live payment gateway.
- No smart-glasses SDK or device pairing.
- Mermaid diagrams in this folder explain plans. They do not replace your HTA flowcharts.
- Phase 3 needs at least five real participants and at least two data-collection techniques, with qualitative and quantitative results. This Week 5 interview constraint does not remove that later requirement.
- Usability testing needs a consent process your lecturer accepts. Do not create participant records in advance.

## 9. Personal data

When Phase 3 starts, store consent and notes only for real participants. Use participant codes in working files. Do not place signed consent forms or identifiable notes in a public remote.

## 10. Submission hygiene

The file you upload is the official Word template, completed by you, plus whatever appendices the template allows. Markdown in this repository is the workshop copy. `submissions/` holds the exported file after you have checked every claim against the source register.

Signature and date on the Week 5 declaration are yours.
