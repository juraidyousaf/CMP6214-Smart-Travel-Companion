## Card

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Label | Accessible Pedestrian Wayfinding |
| Identity | Composite based on secondary research – not a real participant |
| Population, as the source names it | People who are blind, have low vision, or are deafblind and use a smart device for wayfinding. These are not treated as identical in ability, sensory preference, or guidance needs |
| Context | S007: wayfinding with a smart device in real indoor and outdoor settings. 33 of 35 studies used a smart device. **Proposed transfer, not a finding:** this perspective may matter for a city tourist on foot. S007 is not a tourism study |
| Behaviour | S007 synthesis, as review findings, not one person’s routine: control of the speed and amount of guidance; a choice of audio, visual, and haptic output; a preview before travel and live information during travel; a device that stays usable while the person moves. Real-time route information was associated with higher success in the studies reviewed. Parker et al. (2021), S007. Awaiting your verification |
| Load | S007: some participants found apps reduced what they had to remember. Others reported more cognitive load. Both findings are kept. They are not attributed to one individual |
| Travel aid | S007: navigation applications in the review supplement existing mobility aids, such as a cane or a guide dog. They do not replace those aids. This is not a personal history, and it does not say every participant uses the same aid |
| More than one app | Some blind and deafblind travellers in focus groups cited by S007 needed more than one application for one trip because the applications were not integrated. This is about those focus groups. It is not generalised to all users, and it is not stated for every low-vision traveller |
| S006 | Review evidence only, not a biography: there is no single accessible interface, and sensory, cognitive, and physical access need separate attention. Redundant sound or vibration is discussed where sight is limited. Ruginski et al. (2022), S006. Awaiting your verification. Wheelchair routing is not on this card |
| S009 | Competitor context only. Google’s Android help documents TalkBack, spoken distance to the next walking turn, text size, one-hand zoom and pan, a wheelchair-accessible transit filter, and place attributes for entrance, restroom, seating, parking, and elevator in certain countries only. Lens with TalkBack is not supported on tablet-size devices. This composite is not shown to use Google Maps, and the page is not evidence that the features succeed for every user. S009. Awaiting your verification |
| Goal | **Your interpretation of S007, not a participant quotation and not a universal requirement for everyone with visual impairments:** control how wayfinding guidance is provided, with a preview before moving and updates while moving |
