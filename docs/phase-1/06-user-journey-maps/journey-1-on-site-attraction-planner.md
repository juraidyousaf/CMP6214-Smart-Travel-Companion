## Job this map follows

**When** I have arrived with attractions already planned,  
**I want to** look at that plan again and consider places near a main attraction,  
**so I can** select or adjust which attractions to visit, using the earlier plan as the starting point.

## Research evidence

- **Before the Trip** rests on Liu et al. (2020), S015: in 21 interviews, attractions were roughly planned beforehand, using social media and official channels. Not a walking study. Most respondents were of Asian origin. That limit belongs to the sample, not to a nationality for this persona. Awaiting your verification.
- **After Arrival** adds Huertas and Orden-Mejía (2022), S016: 371 of 402 people (92.2%) reported searching for attractions at the destination. Online recall of a last trip, recruited through university contacts. No app was observed. Social networks and apps were reported less often than Google for attraction searches. Awaiting your verification.
- **Near a Main Attraction** rests on S015 only: the same interviews included places near a planned must-see. No channel is named for that moment. Awaiting your verification.

## Design interpretations

- All three goals.
- The JTBD outcome, “select or adjust which attractions to visit.”
- Placing social media’s approve-or-reject role in After Arrival. S015 does not say when that check happened. It is a possible influence in a small sample, not a step every tourist takes, and not a finding that social media was the main attraction channel.
