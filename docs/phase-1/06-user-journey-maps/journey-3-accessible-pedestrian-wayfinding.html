Accessible Pedestrian Wayfinding

Composite based on secondary research – not a real participant.

Wording approved – source verification pending. S007 has not been marked as read.

Job. An interpretation based on secondary research, not a participant statement. When I am wayfinding with a smart device, before I move and while I am moving, I want to adjust the speed and amount of guidance, choose among audio, visual, and haptic output, preview the route before I move, and receive live route updates while I move, so I can access wayfinding information in a manner suited to my needs throughout the journey. The outcome is a design interpretation. S007 does not state it.

S007 review themes

  • Parker et al. (2021), awaiting personal verification. People who are blind, have low vision, or are deafblind. These are not one person’s experience. Real indoor and outdoor settings. Not a tourist study.
  • Preview before travel. Live information during travel. A device that stays usable while the person moves.
  • Speed, amount, and a choice of audio, visual, or haptic output are reported options, not a sequence, and not evidence that every person uses all three.

Kept outside both goals

  • Real-time route information was associated with higher success in the studies reviewed. That association is not either goal and not a personal result for this composite.
  • Some participants found apps reduced what they had to remember. Others reported more cognitive load. Neither finding is assigned to this composite.

Interpretations

  • Both goals, and JTBD-3 as a whole, including the outcome about accessing wayfinding information in a manner suited to the person’s needs.
  • Any proposed application to a city tourist on foot is an interpretation, not an S007 finding.
  • Not inside the research stages: S006, S008, S009, mobility aids as stages, premium purchase, or smart glasses. The block below is hypothetical. Smart Travel Companion’s accessibility has not been tested or validated.
