## Aim

Build a sourced account of how people find places and walk in unfamiliar cities, where current products fall short, and what published accessibility and smart-glasses research does and does not support. The account is a working aid. You interpret it and write the assessed checklist.

Feature ideas F1–F3 in `PRD.md` stay hypotheses until you decide, in your own words, that a source supports them.

## Method

1. Search for peer-reviewed HCI, wayfinding, tourism, and accessibility research, plus official product documentation and WCAG 2.2.
2. Open the source. A search-result title is not evidence.
3. Record the source in `research/source-register.md` before using it.
4. Paraphrase what the source says. Keep the sample, setting, and limit attached to the claim.
5. Separate three layers in every note:
   - what the source states
   - what you infer
   - what would still require you to open an app or read a paywalled paper
6. Compare Google Maps, Wanderlog, and Tripadvisor using official documentation first, then your own observation checklist. Do not invent session notes.
7. You write the introduction, market summary, competitor summary, personas, jobs, journeys, MoSCoW list, and HTAs.

## Inclusion

Prefer a source when it is:

- peer-reviewed, or an official standard, or official product documentation
- about pedestrian wayfinding, tourist information, accessibility of navigation, or hands-free and AR guidance
- open enough that the claim can be checked

Set a source aside when it is mainly about driving, when only a snippet was seen, or when the full text could not be opened. Say that it was set aside.
